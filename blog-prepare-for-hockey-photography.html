Player photography guide

Prepare for better hockey photos without changing how you play.

The strongest images come from real effort and awareness—not posing during live action. These steps help Triangle-area players and families set useful priorities before the game.

Published August 8, 2026 · Groissl Hockey Photography

A player should never chase the camera during a hockey game. The photographer’s job is to find the athlete, anticipate the play, and capture the moment without becoming a distraction. Preparation still matters, but it happens before the puck drops.

Send the jersey number and position

Start with the player’s jersey number, team colors, position, and expected line. If the team uses similar warmup gear or alternate jerseys, mention that too. Goalies should note whether they expect to start, rotate, or dress as backup.

This information helps the photographer identify the player quickly through glass, traffic, and fast changes.

Choose the purpose of the gallery

A recruiting update, family keepsake, senior-night gallery, and social-media set may overlap, but they do not emphasize exactly the same moments. Share the primary use in one sentence.

  • Recruiting or player profile: prioritize visibility, skating posture, puck control, and clean action.
  • Family gallery: balance action with reactions, teammates, and bench personality.
  • Season milestone: include warmups, environment, and moments that establish the occasion.

Play normally and keep your head in the game

The best photos come from authentic hockey. Do not look for the camera during a shift or alter a play to create an image. Strong edge work, active positioning, communication, and competing through the full sequence naturally produce better frames.

Between shifts, normal focus and teammate interaction often create excellent portrait-style images. There is no need to force a pose unless a specific portrait is planned separately.

Make equipment details easy to read

Before warmups, check that the jersey is fully visible and equipment is game-ready. A folded number or loose practice layer can make identification harder. This is not about looking perfect; it is about keeping the player recognizable in a fast environment.

Understand what a photographer cannot guarantee

No photographer can promise a goal, save, celebration, or unobstructed view. Ice time, rink layout, glass, officials, other players, and the flow of the game all affect coverage. A player-focused request increases attention on the athlete, but the final gallery still reflects what happens during the game.

Review the full gallery, not only the obvious action

Families often download the puck-carrying images first and overlook the quieter frames. Look for posture, eye line, defensive positioning, bench emotion, and the small details that show how the athlete competes. Those images can be especially useful in a season recap or personal profile.

Player preparation checklist

  • Send jersey number, team color, position, and expected line.
  • Explain the main purpose of the gallery.
  • Share one or two priority moments, not a huge shot list.
  • Confirm the date, rink, and start time.
  • Play normally and let the photographer follow the game.

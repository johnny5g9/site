Triangle tournament guide

A practical checklist for multi-game hockey photography.

Tournament weekends move across games, rinks, and schedule changes. A simple coverage plan keeps Raleigh, Cary, Wake Forest, and Triangle-area teams from losing the moments they meant to capture.

Published August 8, 2026 · Groissl Hockey Photography

Multi-game hockey photography is not just a longer version of a single-game booking. The schedule changes, players rotate, fatigue affects the game, and the best story often develops across the entire weekend. Good planning protects the photographer’s time and creates a more balanced final gallery.

Send one complete schedule

Share all confirmed game dates, start times, rink locations, and opponents in one message. If the tournament spans multiple Triangle cities, make the travel sequence clear. Identify which games are guaranteed and which depend on standings or advancement.

Choose one team contact who can send updates. Schedule changes scattered across several text threads are easy to miss.

Rank the games by priority

Not every game needs the same emphasis. A team may care most about the opener, a rivalry matchup, an elimination game, or the final. Mark those priorities before the weekend begins. If time or access becomes limited, the photographer can protect the most important coverage first.

Build a player list that is realistic

For full-team coverage, send the roster and jersey numbers. If several families are sharing the booking, identify priority players and make sure the list is manageable. A clear group of priorities produces better coverage than a long set of conflicting requests received during the game.

Confirm access at every location

Access can vary by facility, event, and game. Confirm where the photographer can stand, whether credentials are needed, and who can approve restricted areas. Never assume that access granted at one rink or game will apply everywhere during the tournament.

Plan for variety across the weekend

A three-game gallery should not feel like the same frame repeated. Early games can establish the team and key players. Later games can emphasize reactions, fatigue, pressure, and the evolving story of the weekend.

A useful tournament mix includes:

  • Wide action that shows spacing and game context.
  • Tight player frames and puck battles.
  • Goalie sequences and defensive moments.
  • Bench focus, teammate interaction, and celebrations.
  • Atmosphere and detail images that separate one game from another.

Agree on delivery before the first game

Decide whether the team needs selected images during the weekend or one complete gallery afterward. Fast social-media selects and a fully edited final gallery are different workflows. If immediate images are important, request that before the event rather than between games.

Triangle tournament checklist

  • Send the complete schedule and every rink location.
  • Name one contact for changes.
  • Rank the highest-priority games.
  • Provide roster and jersey numbers.
  • Confirm photographer access at each location.
  • Clarify whether weekend selects are needed.
  • Confirm final photo count, price, and delivery timing.

With those details handled early, the photographer can focus on the hockey instead of chasing logistics between games.
